## Project 1: The Empathy Map

**The challenge:** A fitness startup wants to develop an app for busy office professionals who struggle to find time to exercise. Before designing any screens, the goal was to understand the users' challenges, behaviours, and needs.

**What I created:**
- Problem identification: understanding why existing fitness apps may not suit busy office professionals.
- An illustrative user persona, Alex Morgan, focused on mental clarity and stress relief rather than weight loss.
- A four-quadrant empathy map: Says, Thinks, Does, and Feels.
- Potential pain points, core needs, and design opportunities.

**Key lesson:** Good UX starts with understanding people, not pixels.

## Project 2: The Blueprint (Wireframing)

**The challenge:** An e-commerce brand was losing customers because its homepage layout was cluttered and confusing. The goal was to create a clean, logical homepage structure with a clear user flow and content hierarchy.

**What I created:**
- A low-fidelity homepage wireframe for STRIDE, a conceptual sneaker store.
- A clear hierarchy with a benefit-driven headline and one primary CTA.
- Five product categories and four best sellers to reduce unnecessary choices.
- Distinct primary and secondary button styles.
- Early trust-building elements, including shipping information, returns, and customer reviews.
- Stock availability indicators on product cards.

**Key lesson:** Structure comes before style. A clear wireframe creates a strong foundation for the final visual design.

## Project 3: The Visual Identity

**The challenge:** A premium organic food delivery service wanted a modern, "fresh" look that communicates trust, health, and speed through color and fonts. The goal was to turn wireframes into a high-fidelity UI.

**What I created:**
- A Style Tile for Fieldly with an eight-color palette (Leaf Green, Earthy Brown, and Harvest Gold as the core) and a Playfair Display + Montserrat type pairing.
- Three high-fidelity screens: Splash Screen, Product List, and Product Details.
- Trust-building details such as organic certification badges, harvest time, and a "Rainy Day Comforts" suggestion.
- A consistent 8-point spacing grid and accessible contrast ratios (WCAG AA).

**Key lesson:** Visual design is not decoration. It is persuasion.
